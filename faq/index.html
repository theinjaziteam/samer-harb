General

A Certified Food Scientist (CFS) is a professional credential awarded by the Institute of Food Technologists (IFT) to individuals who have demonstrated mastery of food science knowledge across a wide range of disciplines — including food chemistry, microbiology, food safety, sensory science, and food processing.

Unlike a nutritionist or dietitian who focuses on the health and dietary impacts of food, a Certified Food Scientist is trained in the full technical lifecycle of food — from raw ingredients and processing to quality control, safety standards, and shelf life.

Samer Harb was the first Lebanese Certified Food Scientist, earning the designation in 2019 after meeting IFT's rigorous academic, professional experience, and examination requirements.

This is one of the most frequently asked questions. While both professions deal with food, they serve very different purposes:

  • A Food Scientist focuses on the technical, scientific, and engineering aspects of food: how it is produced, processed, preserved, packaged, and kept safe. Their work involves food chemistry, microbiology, quality systems (like ISO 22000), product development, shelf life testing, and regulatory compliance.
  • A Nutritionist or Dietitian focuses on the relationship between food and human health. They work with individuals or populations to provide dietary advice, meal planning, and health promotion. Their concern is what food does to the body, not how the food is made.

In practical terms: when a food company launches a new product, a Food Scientist ensures it is safe, shelf-stable, properly labeled, and manufactured to standard — while a Nutritionist might advise consumers on whether they should eat it.

ISO 22000:2018 is the international standard for Food Safety Management Systems (FSMS). Getting certified involves:

  • Gap analysis of your current food safety practices against the standard
  • Developing or updating HACCP plans, prerequisite programs (PRPs), and food safety policies
  • Staff training and awareness sessions
  • Internal audit preparation and documentation review
  • Support during the external certification audit

As a certified ISO 22000:2018 Lead Auditor (IRCA-UK), I can guide your facility from gap analysis all the way through to certification — and support ongoing compliance afterward.

Nutritional facts can be derived through several methods depending on your budget, product type, and target market:

  • Calculation-based — using certified food composition databases (e.g., USDA, NUTTAB) to calculate nutrients from ingredients. Faster and more cost-effective for straightforward products.
  • Laboratory analysis — sending product samples to an accredited food lab. More accurate for complex or novel products, and often required for regulatory compliance in certain markets.
  • Combination approach — calculations validated by targeted lab tests for key nutrients.

I handle the full process: methodology selection, calculations, formatting, and ensuring compliance with the specific regulations of your target market (Lebanon, Saudi Arabia, EU, FDA, Health Canada, etc.).
